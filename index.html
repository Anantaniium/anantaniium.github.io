03 / 09THE PROFILE

ABOUT

Between discovery
and opportunity.

A research path shaped by curiosity about the universe—and by a parallel interest in who gets to participate in its exploration.

I am interested in two connected questions: how we understand the universe, and how more people can take part in that work.

I am currently a Master's student in the Paris Physics Master at Université Paris Cité. My research experience spans Fast Radio Burst scintillation analysis at the California Institute of Technology, cosmological modelling at Miranda House, and gamma-ray source studies at the AstroParticle and Cosmology Laboratory in Paris.

Alongside research, I work on the educational and institutional pathways through which students encounter physics. I founded the Indian Network of Physics Students (INPS) and have worked on student-led conferences, academic exchange, science outreach and educational initiatives in India. As Vice President of IAPS, I contribute to international advocacy, outreach, student representation and cooperation across the wider scientific community.

These experiences have shaped my interest in physics education research, science communication, science diplomacy and the role of science in public policy. I am interested not only in how scientific knowledge is produced, but also in how it is taught, shared and made accessible.

My academic direction remains rooted in astrophysics, with longer-term interests in multi-messenger astronomy. Alongside that research trajectory, I hope to contribute to stronger educational pathways and institutions that connect scientific talent with opportunity, particularly in communities and regions that remain underrepresented in international science.

“Research is funded through public money, so people should understand where that money is going.”

— Anant Singh, Careers360 · October 2026
